\section{Option Setup}

\begin{frame}{What is a European put?}

A European put option gives the holder the right, but not the obligation, to sell an asset at a fixed strike price \(K\) at expiry time \(T\).


\begin{itemize}
    \item \(K\): strike price
    \item \(T\): expiry time
    \item \(S_T\): asset price at expiry
\end{itemize}


European means the option can only be exercised at expiry.

\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Payoff at expiry}

At expiry, the payoff of a put is

\[
P(S_T,T)=\max(K-S_T,0).
\]


So if \(S_T<K\), the holder sells at \(K\) when the market price is lower.

\[
\text{payoff}=K-S_T
\]

If \(S_T\geq K\), the option is not exercised.

\[
\text{payoff}=0
\]

\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Why this becomes a pricing problem}

The payoff is easy to calculate at expiry.



Before expiry, the option still has value because the asset price can change before \(T\).



So instead of only knowing

\[
P(S_T,T)=\max(K-S_T,0),
\]

we want to find the value at earlier times:

\[
P(S,t), \qquad 0\leq t<T.
\]



This value depends on \(S,t,K,T,r,\sigma\), and Black-Scholes gives the PDE for it.

\end{frame}
